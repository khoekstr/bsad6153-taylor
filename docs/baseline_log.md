## Reflection

1. **The three most frustrating or time-consuming parts:**
- I prefer to get my news from videos and podcasts and so for that I tend to use YouTube.  I have a couple people I know will be good, but I'll consume more than that.  I never know if a new person is real or AI, has new or good information, or is just clickbait.  The thumb nails that look the most outrageous used to be terrible, but I can not longer tell based on that. I trust information from people I follow on Linked In slightly more than random people on youtube, but those are usually long posts are articles and I don't want to read that much. 

- At least for YouTube, it does pretty good about not giving you a video you have already seen in your feed.  But it does keep giving you the same content.  When looking for printed articles, the time to find it takes a lot more effort and a little more time.  Titles don't always summarize the content well.

- When looking at Open Alex for the first time there were thousands of research papers across significantly more AI subcategories than I was expecting.  I realize I'll need to narrow my interests significantly to get an meaningful initial group to consider for my database. 

2. **What would make me trust a system's recommendation? What would make me ignore it?**
- I would like to know which are recommended by experts in the field, or have large real and respectable followings, or have some sort of accreditation or trustworthiness rating.  

- Trust requires some level of trial and error.  For example, if I have AI rate a research article on a 1 to 5 scale for 'relevance to my interests' and it rates a 5 for a bunch of stuff I don't find interesting... and more importantly can't adjust based on new feedback, then I would lose confidence. 

- Knowing that it really did understand what I was looking for.  I asked ChatGPT to create a multiple choice quiz for me to help determine what I was interested in.  It categorized the results as AI & Society, AI, Learning & Cognition, AI Ethics & Governance, then pulled 10 articles for me to sample.  After reading the abstracts, there were a few sort of interesting but nothing that made me want to read the whole thing.  I provided my thoughts, and questions the abstract raised. ChatGPT used that to give me just 3 articles and they were all very interesting.  I would need my database/website to take my feedback in a similar way. 

3. **What a system should never do on my behalf without asking:**
- Edit or make changes to anything.  May access and read what I have connected it to (i.e. folder), but ask permission before making changes. Is never allowed to see my passwords or access financial information. Specific to my decision, it should never subscribe or comment on a post. 

4. **How I would know it was actually helping** (this becomes my value hypothesis):
I have a few ideas...
- Reduced number of times I click on something start reading or watching and then have to stop because it was not good or something I already new.
- Increased confidence that the news source is trustworthy, and human reviewed/validated.
- Widened the aperture of my news feed to see some new things
- Still seeing trending topics, enough to stay current and get a feel  for all sides of an issue or debate, without it being repetitive.  I.e. I don't want to say not news on 'misbehavior,' I just don't want to click on 'AI just broke out' and it's talking hugging face again, but I don't want to risk missing new things, like the PHP issues identified this week.  
- If I find myself want to read an whole research paper and not just the abstract
- If I find myself wanting to connect information I read from different research papers, or return to one I previously read

5. **One assumption about my own workflow that this log proved wrong:**
- Not really an assumption, but I did realize that what I want isn't just new information, I want something that maybe tracks the types of news I've already ingested and maybe gives me some sort of rating or 'newness to me' factor a recommended piece is.
- As for an assumption, last week I felt I was a bit over it, like all my AI news is old and a waste of time (hence why I though of this question).  But just as I went to record for this log thinking it would take me 5-10 minutes to find something interesting, I took the risk and clicked on something that said "This is Very Serious" with a pretty bad actor looking scared. I thought for sure I'd turn it off and keep scrolling, but was actually about the PHP issues and sort of tied that to Banks identifying agents accessing customer accounts.  I assumed it was going to be a bad article, it was actually new to me, but I don't know if I trust it.  Couldn't the bank issues just be people letting their own agents have access to their own account? 
- I thought I wanted to focus on news, but as I use the log and realize the time I take to find something and read it... what if one day I want to go back and reference something, or I want to keep a note about something or I want to synthesize all my thoughts into a research proposal?  I got to thinking maybe I want to focus more on personal knowledge management and a mix between research discovery and news. 
- I assumed I had to sit back and weight for someone else to post something or create a video for it to find it's way to my feed, but I'm learning I can go to the source and scour through research directly. 

- Bottomline is my workflow isn't working because I have little confidence in the authenticity of the news I get.  Also, I learned, a focus on 'news' sources has me missing a lot of stuff that isn't even making it into the news.
